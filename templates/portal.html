{# ============================== FUNDING ============================== #}

How you fund it

Two ways in, one balance. CHOR is the token; the balance you spend is USD.

🧩
funding · hero

Stake CHOR

Lock CHOR in the portal pallet and receive a rolling free-service allowance, proportional to the amount staked, issued at a fixed published rate. Your stake is never reduced by use — it is a membership right, not a drawdown. Unstake anytime to recover your CHOR.

  • ▸ Allowance refreshes every period based on what you hold
  • ▸ Fixed published rate — predictable, regardless of yield variance
  • ▸ Unused allowance does not roll over
💳
funding · on-ramp

Convert to credits

Convert CHOR to USD Portal credits at deposit time, at the on-chain CHOR/USD rate. No lockup, no position — the low-friction way to start and to top up as you go.

  • ▸ Denominated in USD — you budget in real money
  • ▸ Non-refundable, and never expire
  • ▸ Rate read on-chain, so each conversion is auditable

Spend order

Both inputs feed the same USD balance. When you spend, your free stake allowance is drawn down first, then your credits — so staked CHOR always gets first use before the credits you bought.

per token

per byte-month

per query
{# ============================== PRICING ============================== #}

Priced in USD, funded in CHOR

Services are priced in USD — the same unit the underlying infrastructure (GPU time, storage and bandwidth, indexer servers) is bought in. CHOR is the funding and settlement token. The on-chain CHOR/USD rate is read at deposit time, so every conversion is auditable on-chain and no single company controls the price.

  • ▸ USD price sheet — stable, comparable to other providers
  • ▸ CHOR settles — the token is how you fund, not a volatile meter
  • ▸ On-chain rate — verifiable, not a vendor promise

{# ============================== PORTAL vs CONSENSUS ============================== #}

A portal pallet, not consensus staking

The Portal is deliberately a separate mechanism from the chain's NPoS consensus staking. The two are never conflated.

portal pallet

Service entitlement

Holds CHOR locked by users and issues an allowance receipt. This is a consumer service gate — anyone using the platform's services participates, not just validators.

NPoS consensus

Chain security

Secures the chain and pays validators and nominators for that security. The yield it generates is what backs the free stake allowance — decentralized, not a centralized subsidy.

Consensus staking exists to secure the network. Portal access is a service entitlement for its users. They produce different returns, serve different actors, and live at different layers — so they are separate. The yield from running staked CHOR through NPoS is what funds the free allowance; the portal pallet's lock is what holds it.
