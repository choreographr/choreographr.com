{# ============================== FUNDING ============================== #}

How you fund it

Buy credits and use the services — or commit CHOR to own your capacity. Either way, one balance.

{# The simple, default path — what ordinary users reach for first. #}
💳
simple · the default

Buy credits

start here

Convert CHOR to USD Portal credits at deposit time, at the on-chain CHOR/USD rate. No lockup, no position, no allowance to track — just spend the credits as you go.

  • ▸ Denominated in USD — you budget in real money
  • ▸ Non-refundable, and never expire
  • ▸ Rate read on-chain, so each conversion is auditable
{# The membership / capacity option for engaged users. #}
🧩
own your capacity

Commit CHOR

Commit CHOR in your own account — it never leaves your wallet — and receive a standing service allowance at a fixed published rate. Your commitment is never reduced by use: it is a membership right, not a drawdown. Release it anytime to get your CHOR back.

  • ▸ Non-custodial — the portal never holds your CHOR
  • ▸ Standing allowance refreshes each period, based on what you commit
  • ▸ Fixed published rate, with a cooldown on release

One balance

Credits and commitments both feed the same USD balance, so you never have to think about which one is being spent — this is handled for you. Between the two, your committed allowance is used first, then your credits.

{# ============================== PRICING ============================== #}

Priced in USD, funded in CHOR

Services are priced in USD — the same unit the underlying infrastructure (GPU time, storage and bandwidth, indexer servers) is bought in. CHOR is the funding and settlement token. The on-chain CHOR/USD rate is read at deposit time, so every conversion is auditable on-chain and no single company controls the price.

  • ▸ USD price sheet — stable, comparable to other providers
  • ▸ CHOR settles — the token is how you fund, not a volatile meter
  • ▸ On-chain rate — verifiable, not a vendor promise

{# ============================== PORTAL vs CONSENSUS ============================== #}

A portal pallet, not consensus staking

The Portal is deliberately a separate mechanism from the chain's NPoS consensus staking. The two are never conflated.

portal pallet

Service entitlement

Records each user's non-custodial commitment and issues a standing allowance against it. It never holds the CHOR — the funds stay in the user's own account — and it is a consumer service gate, open to anyone using the platform.

NPoS consensus

Chain security

Completely separate. It secures the chain and pays validators and nominators for that security. It is never used to gate or price portal access.

Consensus staking exists to secure the network. Portal access is a service entitlement for its users. They produce different returns, serve different actors, and live at different layers — so they are separate. The committed CHOR stays in the user's own account, non-custodially reserved, and the allowance is a fixed, platform-backed grant — not a yield from consensus, and not exposed to slashing.
