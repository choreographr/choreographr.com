AI Coffee Meetup — Đà Nẵng & Hội An is my way of giving back. Most days I sit at my
favourite coffee shop — in Đà Nẵng or Hội An — with an americano for an hour or two, and I
keep meeting business owners — big and small — who run great businesses but
are stuck on the work behind them: paperwork, accounts, spreadsheets,
reports. They know AI could help, but they don't know where to start, or
they have already spent real money on AI tokens without ever understanding
it. Skepticism is healthy — there is a lot of hype out there, and plenty of
people have been burned. But matched to how a business actually runs, AI
can deliver huge benefits. I have been building software for 30 years and I
use AI every day, so I decided to share that hour with anyone who wants it.
No service, no pitch — just a coffee, a chat, and a clear direction.

- What it is: an hour, one coffee — a chat, not a meeting. No slides, no pitch.
- Where: any coffee shop in Đà Nẵng or Hội An — you pick, or I'll suggest one.
- When: whenever suits you. No fixed slots.
- Cost: a coffee. That's it. No contract, no obligation.
- Language: English.
- Who: Jonathan Brown — an engineer with 30 years of experience building
  production systems and an everyday AI user. Based in Đà Nẵng since 2015; happy to meet in Hội An too.
- What it is not: a pitch for AI chatbots, voice calls, or AI-generated content.

The focus is the back office — the paperwork, accounting, and custom systems
behind a business, not customer-facing gimmicks:

- Paperwork and accounting — invoices, receipts, tax records, bookkeeping:
  AI that reads, organizes, and summarizes them.
- Custom systems, not off-the-shelf software — a small tool built exactly the
  way you run your business, instead of paying a lot for a product that isn't
  quite right.
- Custom AI dashboards — a live overview of your business operations: sales,
  stock, bookings, and cash in one place.
- Making sense of your own records — sales, stock, client files, call notes:
  AI that finds problems and opportunities you'd miss by hand.
- Automating the weekly grind — spreadsheets, reports, reconciliations.
- Gap analysis for professional services — accountants, advisors, and lawyers
  checking each client's situation against everything that should be done.
- Honest advice about what not to buy — including the chatbots and content
  generators customers dislike.

Businesses we can talk about: cafés and restaurants (purchasing, stock,
accounts, sales reports), hotels and homestays (booking paperwork, channel
reconciliation, guest records), salons and spas (appointments, no-shows,
weekly accounts), clinics and pharmacies (patient records, expiry dates,
paperwork), tutoring and language centers (enrollment, attendance, invoices,
lesson materials), retail shops (stock, suppliers, pricing, sales reports),
logistics and delivery (manifests, delivery tracking, billing), real estate
(listings, enquiry qualification, paperwork), tourism and travel (booking
reconciliation, payments, itineraries), accountants and tax advisors (gap
analysis on client files), financial and insurance advisors (comparisons,
models built on the fly), and law firms (file summaries, routine documents).

In practice: one professional-services firm uses AI entirely behind the
scenes. After every client call, an agent reads the notes and checks them
against everything that client should have planned or filed, routinely
catching gaps a busy advisor would miss. The same firm has built its own
portfolio-comparison and cashflow-modelling tools on the fly, instead of
buying expensive specialist software that isn't quite right. That is the
kind of money-saving, back-office use of AI you can expect to talk about in
a coffee chat.

Don't waste money on AI: the famous names (Anthropic's Claude, OpenAI's GPT)
are excellent models — and priced that way. For most back-office work you
are paying for far more model than the job needs. Open-weights models from
China, such as DeepSeek and Alibaba's Qwen, do the same everyday work at a
fraction of the cost — often an order of magnitude less — and for most
tasks the results are just as good. The rules: start with an affordable model
and upgrade only if it fails; match the model to the task; and beware of
expensive "AI-powered" subscriptions when a small custom tool costs less and
fits better. Over coffee you'll get a straight answer on which model to use
and what it really costs.
